Basira Hussain is a police officer who has signed a [[Section 31]] form as she stated in episode 43, which means that she has investigated cases with supernatural elements in the past, and will be called upon when other cases turn weird.

# Personality

Basira initially presents herself in season 2 as isolated and fairly powerless, avoided by other police officers due to the stigma of the [[Section 31]] status and left without adequate support to properly investigate the murder of [[Gertrude Robinson]] (lacking even basic resources like a tape recorder to listen to the tapes found around her body). While this is a ruse to a degree, as Basira was using the tapes as to encourage her main suspect not to flee, by the end of the season she has witnessed the Metropolitan Police covering up _two_ other officers being killed by [[Avatar|avatars]], quitting because of this. She demonstrates an ability to conceal her feelings about people, as [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is surprised to learn that he was her prime suspect, but she does also come to genuinely like [[Jonathan Sims|John]] despite suspecting him of murder. 

She wants to avoid the paranormal after her traumatic experiences, but her personal connection to [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] drags her back in. Her initial reaction to [[Elias Bouchard|Elias']] threat is passivity: she reads, and sometimes justifies this as theoretically finding clues for escape. But she does genuinely seem comfortable in her information gathering state, infodumping about the results of her reading to [[Martin Blackwood|Martin's]] confusion. She bonds quickly with [[Melanie King|Melanie]], and reaches out to [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] somewhat, but has no significant interactions with [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]]. Like [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]], she is not moved to seriously strategise against [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] until [[Melanie King|Melanie]] is threatened.

This marks an important development in her journey, as she has so far been mostly falling on the "accept it and adapt" side of her father's dichotomy, and is now trying to "fight and change it". She carries this into the gap between season 3 and 4, during which the extreme multifaceted stress she is under causes a significant hardening of her affect.

In her final season, Basira fully confronts the systemic failings of policing and her own contribution to them. She comes to grip with the fact that she stood by and supported ordinary miscarriages of justice, and unravels the idea both that if someone is guilty then it is just to hurt them and whether anyone can reliably judge who counts as guilty. Reflecting on the same time period that in season 2 she describes feelings of powerlessness, she now perceives herself as having had power and enjoying it. Her final conclusion is ambiguity, and wanting to make important decisions by involving more of the people directly affected, with no bright lines short of genocide being definitely wrong.

In general, Basira tries to approach the world through a logical, rational point of view, in contrast to the dream logic of the fears. Sometimes this is beneficial to her, especially in facing [[The Stranger]], but it also leads her to misread situations and misjudge the costs of certain actions. She is also simultaneously impulsive, as a way of seizing control in the face of repeated traumas; she doesn't really want to make a plan.

Similar to her reading material, Basira was a fan of [[Georgie Barker]]'s nonfiction podcast [[What The Ghost]] (or at least the early seasons), while she wasn't particularly interested in The Archers. She took a welding class once, on a whim, and burned herself by touching the metal while it was still hot. Her home was less nice than a run down apartment block in Kensington.

She had a similar dry sense of humour to [[Jonathan Sims|John]], with a slight tendency to puns. She describes paranormal phenomena through impromptu onomatopoeia.

# History

## Season Two

Basira is in charge of the case of [[Gertrude Robinson's]] murder but does not get much assistance from the rest of the police force, lest they be branded with [[Section 31]] as well. As part of this, she has been providing [[Jonathan Sims]] with some of the tapes recovered from the room where [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude's]] body was found.

[[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] believes that Basira and [[Jonathan Sims|Jonathan]] are in a relationship, a lie that they agree to uphold to explain her infrequent visits. Basira's partner [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] later explains that John was under investigation for [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude's]] death and that the tapes were initially being delivered in order to explain away Basira's investigation. CCTV later exonerates [[Jonathan Sims|John]], and according to [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]], Basira was still delivering tapes out of sympathy.

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] later goes to visit Basira to ask for more tapes, where she tells him that she is now under scrutiny for assisting him and that they both suck at spy-craft.

She later phones [[Jonathan Sims|John]] asking him for advice before they move in to assault a compound where [[Maxwell Rayner]] is located. Following the raid, she gives a statement to him about the raid, where [[Maxwell Rayner|Rayner]] and some other cult members were killed. The events badly shake Basira, and she resigns from the police force, and advises [[Jonathan Sims|John]] to leave his job as well.

## Season Three

Following [[Jonathan Sims|John]] leaving [[The Archives]] and being under suspicion for murder, Basira shows up to interrogate [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] as to his whereabouts. She admits that she liked [[Jonathan Sims|John]] as a friend, but is not as convinced that he could not be a murderer. When she hears that [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] was using full "operational discretion" to hunt him down, she quickly leaves, telling [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] to contact her if he learns about [[Jonathan Sims|John's]] whereabouts.

Basira waits for [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] at the place where [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] "kills monsters", and rescues [[Jonathan Sims|John]] before [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] can execute him. She is present at the confrontation with [[Elias Bouchard]] where she signs an employment contract with [[The Magnus Institute]] in order to avoid the current police officers killing her and [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] to cover up [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy's]] murders.

She is essentially a hostage but finds the role surprisingly comfortable. She investigates statements but also researches the paranormal on her own. She eventually starts plotting against [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] and planning for [[The Unknowing]] with the others.

She accompanies [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]], [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] and [[Jonathan Sims|John]] to the [[House of Wax]] in Great Yarmouth but [[The Unknowing]] starts before they can blow it up. She knows that she is in a place but everything is too much and there are things that want to hurt her. She decides to leave by simply picking a where and moving. Bit by bit, she reasons her way out of [[The Unknowing]] and she is the only one who leaves the waxworks mostly unharmed.

## Season Four

After [[The Unknowing]], [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is in a coma while both [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] and [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] are presumed dead. Basira is left working in the Archives with [[Melanie King|Melanie]] and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]]. The other powers have started paying more attention to the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]] due to their role in stopping [[The Unknowing]] and the fact that, to their knowledge, the [[The Eye|Beholding]] has not attempted a [[Rituals|ritual]] yet.

In December 2017, [[Jared Hopworth]] and his allies launch an assault on the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]]. [[Melanie King|Melanie]] is instrumental in fending them off and saves Basira’s life.

She helps [[Jonathan Sims|John]] perform improvised surgery on [[Melanie King|Melanie]] to remove [[The Slaughter]] bullet and looks after her in the aftermath. Feeling the pressure of protecting the Archives on her own, she agrees to meet with [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] and to hear him out.

She is present when [[Breekon & Hope|Breekon]] delivers [[The Coffin| the coffin]] to the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]]. Even though [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] is in it, she agrees with [[Jonathan Sims|John's]] assessment that they cannot open it. She leaves for 3 weeks to pursue some leads given to her by [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] and in the meantime, [[Jonathan Sims|John]] enters [[The Coffin]]. He emerges after 3 days with [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] and while Basira is relieved to have her back, she is also dismayed by how she has changed. She needs help protecting the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]] and in her current state, [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] is just another person that needs to be protected.

She confronts [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] about the false leads he gave her but [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] points out that her absence leads to the rescue of [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]]. He also warns her of a recent surge of activity in [[Ny-Ålesund]] which seems to imply [[The People's Church of the Divine Host]] is preparing to do a ritual.

Without revealing her source, Basira heads to [[Ny-Ålesund]] with [[Jonathan Sims|John]]. They find no ritual there but encounter [[Manuela Dominguez]] who is guarding [[The Black Sun]], a powerful [[Artefacts|artefact]] of [[The Dark]]. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] destroys [[The Black Sun]] by looking at it and [[Manuela Dominguez|Manuela]] is captured by [[The Distortion|Helen]] when she attempts to escape.

She finds out about [[Jonathan Sims|John]] feeding on innocent peoples' trauma from a tape left for her by [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] and confronts [[Jonathan Sims|John]] alongside the others. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] raises the possibility that he is being controlled by [[The Web]] and she jumps on it, immediately deciding to go to [[Hill Top Road]]. The others all follow. They find a statement which reveals that [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is not being controlled, which he presumably tells Basira about.

Basira visits [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] in prison again. She beats him up and confronts him about the false leads that sent them to [[Ny-Ålesund]], [[Annabelle Cane]], and [[Jonathan Sims|John's]] eating habits. She threatens to have his privileges revoked but he points out that she does not have that power with the police anymore.

As [[Jonathan Sims|John]] struggles to abstain from eating trauma, she gives him an ultimatum; Either he stops, or she kills him.

After [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] and [[Jonathan Sims|John]] tell her about their confrontation with [[Julia Montauk]] and [[Trevor Herbert]], she tries to convince [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] to hunt them so she that will not starve to death.

Basira gets word that [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] has escaped and heads over to check it out with [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]]. They return to find a statement on [[Jonathan Sims|John's]] desk which reveals [[The Panopticon]] under the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]] and that [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] is actually [[Jonah Magnus]]. They decide to head into the tunnels when [[Not-Sasha]], [[Julia Montauk|Julia]], and [[Trevor Herbert|Trevor]] all show up. Basira and [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] stay behind to slow them down as [[Jonathan Sims|John]] heads for [[The Panopticon]]. In a last-ditch effort, [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] gives in to [[The Hunt]], but not before making Basira promise to find and kill her once it is all over.

Basira survives the assault on the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]] but [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] is missing. She keeps in touch with [[Jonathan Sims|John]] and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] while they are in Scotland and says that she will send some statements once the Archives are no longer a crime scene.

## Season Five

After the apocalypse, Basira is not trapped in a static nightmare like most but is pursuing [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]], who is bestial and carving her way through the [[Domains]] of other powers. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] catch up with Basira in a [[Domains|domain]] of [[The Hunt]], where she kills [[Trevor Herbert]].

They pursue [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] together and eventually find her in a [[Domains|domain]] of [[The Desolation]]. She attacks and injures [[Jonathan Sims|John]] but recognises Basira, who is able to talk her down. [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] asks her to join her in [[The Hunt]] but Basira shoots and kills her, fulfiling her promise. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] continue on toward London, but Basira decides to stay behind to mourn and burn the body.

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] discuss Basira's struggles through the apocalypse, noting she's in a hungry void and a labyrinth of masks. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] reveals that she was in fact a sufferer in [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy's]] domain. Eventually she gets stuck on an island. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] picks her up in his boat, and the two head to [[Hill Top Road]], before jumping off a cliff to return to London.

# Trivia

- Basira is voiced by Frank Voss who was in the band [The Mechanisms](https://themechanisms.com/the-crew/) with writer Jonny Sims and fellow voice actors Tim Ledsam ([[Jordan Kennedy]]) and Jessica Law ([[Nikola Orsinov]]).
- Basira once took a welding class.
- Basira's D&D class would be paladin.
- Basira used to listen to [[Georgie Barker]]'s podcast "[[What the Ghost?]]". She loved the first two seasons but thinks it took a weird turn in season three.
- Basira is shown repeatedly to have the ability to reason or brute force her way out of supernatural situations. This is shown when she chooses a direction and goes during [[The Unknowing]] or how she travels unaffected during [[The Change]]. This also shows a degree of separation from [[The Eye]], as [[The Eye]] is unable to understand information given to it.
- In development, Basira's last name was Khan but it was changed to Hussain before recording started. A leftover from this can be heard in the credits of [[MAG 80 - The Librarian]] which refer to her as "PC Basira Khan".
- Basira's first name, as well as her surname "Hussain", are of Arabic and Persian origin.
    - Basira can mean wise or all-seeing in Arabic.

# Statement

Doctor [[Jonathan Fanshawe]] visited [[Albrecht von Closen]]'s estate under the beseeching of [[Jonah Magnus]], the events of which lead him to cease their association. [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] informed [[Jonah Magnus|Magnus]] he would no longer collect and provide him esoteric accounts, and declined his offer of a position at [[Millbank Penitentiary]].

Under the impression that he was to improve [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]]'s illness, [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] travelled to his estate in early April of 1831. Upon arrival, he noted smoke and the glow of a fire, and, despite the rain, when they approached it was to find a gnarled and ancient elm isolated from the forest and set alight.

The tree was surrounded by a crowd, among them what looked to be a groundskeeper, who held a lit torch.

[[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] asked him why they were burning the tree with it raining so heavily, to which the man replied with resignation that his master (whom [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] assumed was [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]]) wanted the tree "dead."

As [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]]'s wife Clara was dead, and his sons at school, the housekeeper Greta greeted [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] at the estate. Greta welcomed him in and apologised that [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]] was indisposed. Despite [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] explaining he was a doctor here to help with his illness, she responded that he would see [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] when he was ready.

Greta left [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] in the drawing room, leaving the door to the corridor open. As he waited, he noted the house seemed empty and devoid of life, and felt the sensation of someone watching him from the corridor.

[[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] continued to feel watched, eating dinner under the eye of an unresponsive Greta, attempting unsuccessfully to find comfort smoking back in the drawing room, and in his room, noticing the light of the candle reflecting off the shutter-less window like an eye.

Eventually [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] managed to sleep, however when he awoke the feeling of being watched had intensified immensely, and he was certain there was something in the room staring at him. Striking a match for light, the face of Albrecht [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]] loomed over him. Von Closen appeared pale and sickly, repeating the phrase "Leg sie alle zurück" (Put them back).

The match burnt out, and after lighting another, [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] found himself alone.

[[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] did not sleep, and at dawn he searched the house for [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]]. Finding him in the library mesmerised by the lit fireplace, [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] shut the door behind him and asked why [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]] had visited him during the night.

[[Albrecht von Closen|Von Closen]] apologised and drew [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]]'s attention to the bookshelves, and told him that due to water damage he had the books rebound last year. Ignoring [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]]'s insistence on discussing his health, [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]] proceeded to tell him a number of stories which transfixed [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] and rid him of the strength to leave despite wanting to. Among the stories, [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] noted of a few. One about a seamstress who removed her skin, one about a man with a terrible fear of death, and one about a fire so hot that even knowing about it was enough to burn a man’s tongue from his head.

Horrified, [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] asked where he had read them. [[Albrecht von Closen|Von Closen]] laughed, touching a spine labelled 'A Warning' and seeming to fight the urge to toss it into the fire. In German, he replied, "I do not read the books. They read me."

As his stay progressed, [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] discovered that [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]] had retrieved the books from [[MAG 23 - Schwartzwald|a tomb]], and began to suspect his mania and sickness was due to a contaminant from the books. Having no time or equipment for tests, he convinced himself that removing the books would help alleviate the situation.

With relief at the suggestion, [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]] asked [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] if he would help, and the two proceeded to transport a library's worth of books through the Black Forest. They travelled to an ancient cemetery, and descended into a mausoleum, placing the books onto their original shelves.

When the last book had been replaced, [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] heard [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]] scream from the top of the stairs, and ran up them to find him dead, though his body undamaged.

[[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] suspected his death was related to his observation while replacing the books - that all of their pages were blank. Later, he checked with [[Jonah Magnus|Magnus]]' preferred book-binders and confirmed that the books returned were not the same as those that were taken. He 'hopes' that the books bring [[Jonah Magnus|Magnus]] much wisdom, as it was a high cost.

Before [[Albrecht von Closen|von Closen]] was buried, [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]] obtained permission to do an autopsy. Covering his organs, bones and inside his skin, dozens of eyes with irises of every hue turned to focus on [[Jonathan Fanshawe|Fanshawe]]. He burned the corpse. He tells [[Jonah Magnus|Magnus]] he knows it was his fault, and asks him not to write to him again.

# Post-Statement

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] muses that he had hoped [[Jonah Magnus]] was innocent in the terrible things that surround the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]] but has now been proven wrong. He is also well aware that the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]] was founded precisely 200 years ago and he feels that _something_ is coming.

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] and [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] talk about [[Melanie King|Melanie]]. She is still shaken but seems to be doing better after they removed the bullet and has asked [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] to convey her apologies for stabbing [[Jonathan Sims|John]]. He notes that his wound has already healed, far quicker than it should have. [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] is uneasy with [[Jonathan Sims|John]]'s growing powers and he struggles to explain them as he does not fully understand them either. He describes that there is an ocean of knowledge behind a door in his mind; if he opens the door, he will drown. [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] also reveals that [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]]'s mother died while [[Jonathan Sims|John]] was away.

At [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]]'s request, [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] visits him in prison. A [[Tape Recorders|tape recorder]] appeared in his cell, which he takes as a sign [[Jonathan Sims|John]] has woken up. [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] points out that he is still able to watch them from his cell; [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] feels it would distract [[Jonathan Sims|John]] from his development if [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] was around him right now. He notes that removing the bullet may have saved [[Melanie King|Melanie]] as a person, but it has rendered her useless as a protector at a time when the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]] is threatened on all sides. He claims to have an idea on how to protect the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]] and [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] agrees to hear him out.

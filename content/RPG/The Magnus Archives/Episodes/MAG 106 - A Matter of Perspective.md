# Statement

[[Jan Kilbride|Jan]]'s statement begins with him explaining how mindblowingly vast the universe is, how small we are and how people cannot even possibly wrap their minds around this concept.

He was chosen as an astronaut for the space station _[[Daedalus]]_ by a [[Simon Fairchild|Mr. Fairchild]], who did not explain why he picked [[Jan Kilbride|Jan]] but referenced his psychiatric profile several times in the interview. He was sent up along with [[Manuela Dominguez]] and [[Carter Chilcott]] ([[MAG 57 - Personal Space]]), though he notes that [[Carter Chilcott|Chilcott]] was doing an isolation study and spent the mission in a separate compartment behind a thick, sturdy door. [[Manuela Dominguez|Dominguez]] also had [[MAG 135 - Dark Matter|her own tasks]], but [[Jan Kilbride|Jan]] suspected he was being given useless busywork replicating experiments that had already been done on the ISS.

Eventually, [[Jan Kilbride|Jan]] began to feel as though they were not alone in space, though when he tried to discuss this feeling with [[Manuela Dominguez|Manuela]] she was dismissive. This feeling grew stronger day after day, until finally the entire station was shaken by a deep, concussive noise loud enough to make [[Jan Kilbride|Jan]]'s ears bleed. He tried to find [[Manuela Dominguez|Manuela]], but when he kicked off a wall his forward momentum abruptly stopped, and he found the nearest hand grips were somehow too far away to reach. He spent an unknown amount of time flailing in the middle of the passageway before [[Manuela Dominguez|Manuela]] reappeared, claiming she had not heard anything.

After this, [[Jan Kilbride|Jan]] spent a lot of time staring out into the void, and the mission controllers stopped sending him work. Eventually he went on a spacewalk to repair one of the station's solar panels, but instead of climbing onto the exterior of the station he just floated farther and farther away from it. Eventually Earth and the station receded to tiny specks, and he was confronted with a vast shadowy shape that blotted out the stars where it passed. He reflected that such a being could easily have killed him without even noticing, just as a human might accidentally tread on a weed.

# Post-Statement

[[Melanie King|Melanie]] is puzzled by the statement's abrupt end, as [[Jan Kilbride|Kilbride]] definitely returned to Earth with [[Carter Chilcott|Chilcott]] and [[Manuela Dominguez|Dominguez]], but she can find no record of him anywhere after that. She remarks that it is like he disappeared into thin air.

[[Basira Hussain|Basira]] interrupts her train of thought by inviting her out for a drink, and they pause to gossip about [[Martin Blackwood|Martin’s]] crush on [[Jonathan Sims|John]]. While on the topic, [[Melanie King|Melanie]] indicates that [[Jonathan Sims|John]] may be asexual; according to [[Georgie Barker|Georgie]], he does not have sex. [[Melanie King|Melanie]] also mentions that [[Jonathan Sims|John]] and [[Georgie Barker|Georgie]] dated many years ago. The two women eventually decide to invite [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] along for drinks. [[Melanie King|Melanie]] offers to talk to him once she has wrapped up the statement.

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]], however, sends [[Melanie King|Melanie]] to [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]], who calls the meeting her first "performance review." He is frustrated by [[Melanie King|Melanie]]'s repeated attempts to kill him, and the fact that he cannot figure out how to make her trust him. Instead, he reveals that [[Melanie King|Melanie]]'s father did not die in his sleep at the [[Ivy Meadows Care Home]] as she had been told. He implants in her mind the knowledge of how he _did_ die, and [[Melanie King|Melanie]] begins to weep. [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] then warns her that if she continues to defy him, he will give her more information on exactly how awful his death was, and implant it so deeply in her mind that she will never be able to stop thinking about it. He then offers her the rest of the day off.

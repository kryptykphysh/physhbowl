# Statement

[[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] is meeting with [[Arthur Nolan]], former leader of [[The Cult of the Lightless Flame]]. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] tells [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] that with [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]] dead and her "woodland circle" broken, if he wanted to kill her, she could not stop him. [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] dismisses this and says he should check on [[Eugene Vanderstock|Eugene]]; she knows where he has lived ever since he tried to intimidate her, and that he has not been attending meetings with the Cult for the last two weeks. She tells [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] to make some calls and then they can continue their discussion. The recorder clicks off.

Sometime later, the recorder clicks back on. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] is noticeably tenser, and asks [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] how she did it. She says he does not need to know how. She muses that, with all the death and destruction she has visited on the Cult over the last 40 years, their master [[The Desolation]], must be quite fond of her. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] calls that blasphemy and [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] tells him he is free to try and leave. He does not.

[[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] speculates that [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] came there to try to use her to restore his standing in the Cult, which has waned since [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]]' death in 2006. He thinks she is vulnerable now, but she has been preparing for this for four decades. She calls the Cult lazy fools who cannot conceive of someone actively working against them. She took care of [[Eugene Vanderstock|Eugene]] and as far as she is concerned, the rest of them are not any more of a threat to her.

That out of the way, [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] is ready to have a real conversation. She offers that [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] may ask her a question first. He asks how did she do what she did to [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]]. She says it was [[The Web]], although she did not know it at the time. She was young, about 25, and the Cult was the first proper [[Rituals|ritual]] attempt she had encountered. She started researching counter-measures and found a tin box in the ashes of the house at [[Hill Top Road]] that contained some of [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]]' hair. She performed what she thought was a banishment ritual, constructing a circle in the middle of a forest in the Scottish highlands, but the ritual actually invited the "Mother of Puppets" and allowed it to bind [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] to [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]], interweaving their existences. It was painful and almost burned [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] from the inside out. She maintained the circle over the years, lacing it with [[The Desolation|Desolation]] symbology to ward off negative side effects. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] says he does not envy whoever broke the circle, though he spent years searching for it, and [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] confirms that it went badly for the person in question.

[[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] asks [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] what [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]] was like. Though their lives had been tied together, [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] never actually met [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]]. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] admits that he did not really know her, or what she really thought about being the Cult's chosen one; he wishes he had taken the time to when she was alive. He says that [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]] was like seeing the sun, with so much power and fire and rage inside her; to look at her was too much for most.

[[Agnes Montague|Agnes]] also never said how she felt about being tied to [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]]; she only called [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] her anchor, the thing trying to hold her down and stop her destiny. [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] wonders why the Cult did not come for her immediately. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] says [[Diego Molina|Diego]] was convinced that [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] dying a violent death would be dangerous for [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]], so the Cult ended up protecting [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] more often than not. They planned to wait out her lifetime, but over that time [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]] began to doubt. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] muses though the various [[Avatar|avatars]] feel their gods' desire to change the world, [[The Entities]] never actually tell them what to do. He thinks they understand people almost as little as people understand them.

[[Agnes Montague|Agnes]] was a person as well as a god, and [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] feels the Cult messed up raising her. He remembers an incident in her childhood when she killed a Cult member who tried to feed her. When [[Diego Molina|Diego]] tried to get the cult members to read a childcare book, [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] saw it as an attack against his leadership and burned it. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] then rants bitterly that [[Diego Molina|Diego]] does not deserve to be the leader of the Cult; [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] insists she does not really care. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] says he has always respected [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] for not caring about the power of the Entities. She focuses on the practical, earthly things that serve the power, which she can actually stop.

[[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] asks about [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]]'s place, now that [[Diego Molina|Diego]] leads the Cult. [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] explains he is now a landlord, and enjoys using that power to torment people, including burning an elderly tenant and her family alive in her mobile home. He mentions that he has purchased a house that hosts an infestation of [[The Corruption]]. [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] interrupts [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]]'s musing on torturing his tenant to end the discussion. She tells him to tell the others what she did to [[Eugene Vanderstock|Eugene]], and that she is extending her protection to [[Jack Barnabas]], [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]]' former romantic partner. If any of the Cult, including [[Jude Perry]], have a problem with that, they are welcome to try to move against her. She then kicks [[Arthur Nolan|Arthur]] out of the Archives.

# Post-Statement

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] muses philosophically on the realities of being an [[Avatar]] of the entities. They have been back in London for a week since he and [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] went to [[Ny-Ålesund]]. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is worried that there are not any answers anywhere to be found, not like he wants there to be.

He did some digging into [[Eugene Vanderstock]] and found out that he disappeared in 2009, leaving behind nothing but a life-size statute of himself made of wax and sawdust and missing its head. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] believes that the statute IS [[Eugene Vanderstock|Eugene]], frozen in place by the sawdust mixed up in his wax flesh. He does not know where [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] stored [[Eugene Vanderstock|Eugene]]'s head.

[[Georgie Barker|Georgie]] interrupts. She is looking for [[Melanie King|Melanie]], who [[Jonathan Sims|John]] says he saw a few hours ago in the other office. He asks why she is here, and she says it is not really her place to discuss it but before she can finish her statement, [[Jonathan Sims|John]] Knows that [[Georgie Barker|Georgie]] is there to take [[Melanie King|Melanie]] to therapy. He forgets that [[Georgie Barker|Georgie]] and [[Melanie King|Melanie]] are friends. [[Melanie King|Melanie]] gets nervous going to therapy alone.

[[Georgie Barker|Georgie]] asks how [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is doing. He says he is doing fine, just trying to rest up. [[Georgie Barker|Georgie]] is doubtful, given that she just heard him talking about a screaming, headless corpse. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] admits that he is deep in it and [[Georgie Barker|Georgie]] thinks he should get some therapy. He sardonically asks if she would go with him as well, and she says no. He thought as much. [[Georgie Barker|Georgie]] awkwardly leaves, telling [[Jonathan Sims|John]] to take care of himself.

# Statement

[[Robert E. Geiger]] is a journalist and has been looking into dust bowls in Oklahoma. The dust storms would sweep across the great plains, wiping out farmlands. [[Stefan Brotchen]] was one such farmer with intense eyes.

[[Robert E. Geiger|Robert]] had gone to Boise City to investigate a series of dust storms which had blown as far as Washington. The city had been founded on fraud in the 1800s when con-men sold the deeds to land they did not own, and the conned people stayed and set up shop. One such person was [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]]'s father.

[[Harry Eisenhard]], [[Robert E. Geiger|Robert]]'s photographer, had heard of [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]]'s situation: his farm decimated by the constant dust storms. The two reporters went to investigate and found [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]]'s farm submerged in dust. Although [[Harry Eisenhard|Harry]] and [[Robert E. Geiger|Robert]] held handkerchiefs to their face to prevent dust pneumonia, when they met [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]] he did not use one.

[[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]] was friendly and explained his situation. When they had gathered all they needed, [[Robert E. Geiger|Robert]] shook his hand, finding it extremely hot. [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]] keeled over, coughing up clods of pulpy brown sludge onto the floor. [[Harry Eisenhard|Harry]] hurried [[Robert E. Geiger|Robert]] out of the building, where they saw a huge dust storm to the west, coming at them at great speed.

[[Robert E. Geiger|Robert]] returned to [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]], planning to ask if he has somewhere they could wait out the storm, but he found the farmer on the floor with a thin trickle of dirt flowing from his mouth and nose.

The two reporters carried [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]] out and placed him in their car. They quickly drove back towards town, but the dust storm caught up with them. The engine clogged up and died and the two men resolved to wait out the storm.

[[Robert E. Geiger|Robert]] believes [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]] died, as he spoke to them through lungs full of dust. He made promises that when the sky fell and became an eternity of mud, he would carve out a part of the [[Forever Buried]] for them.

[[Robert E. Geiger|Robert]] heard [[Harry Eisenhard|Harry]] fighting something, the sound of flesh hitting soil before the door opened. When the storm settled, [[Harry Eisenhard|Harry]] was nowhere to be found, although [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]]'s body was recovered.

# Final Comments

Based on the deceptive origins of Boise City, [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] suspected the city to be connected to [[The Spiral]]. The location at the centre of Dust Bowl territory, however, led her to believe another power is at work.

She claims that the prophecies from [[Stefan Brotchen|Stefan]] were from the dust itself and she has been examining fault lines and seismic data.

[[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] is interrupted by [[Michael Shelley|Michael]], saying he has found the translation for Mr. Vargas's statement. The Archives is having a climate-controlled storage installed over the weekend and [[Michael Shelley|Michael]] plans to help.

[[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] believes that North America is going to be the focus for [[The Buried]]. She is going to try and tie down the location for some [[Rituals|ritual]] and has a plan forming which involves [[Jan Kilbride]]. Although she does almost feel some guilt over [[Jan Kilbride|Jan's]] involvement, she notes it will be 10 more years before she can afford to develop a conscience.

# Trivia

- [[Robert E. Geiger]] was the Associated Press reporter whose account of the "Black Sunday" dust storm on 14th April 1935, which he witnessed along with photographer [[Harry Eisenhard|Harry G. Eisenhard]], led (as rewritten by a news editor) to the coining of the term "Dust Bowl".
- "[[Stefan Brotchen]]" is basically German for [Steve Bread](https://twitter.com/SteveBread), which is the twitter handle used by a friend of writer Jonny Sims. Steve also submitted several questions that were answered in the Q&A episodes for season 2 and 3.

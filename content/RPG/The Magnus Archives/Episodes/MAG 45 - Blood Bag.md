# Statement

[[Thomas Neill]] worked under [[Neil Thompson]], who was looking into ways to cure Malaria, specifically in making a blood substitute that attracts mosquitoes more than people. They had thousands of mosquitoes at the lab that they used for testing. The blood substitute, dubbed 'haemoglobish', was stored in a bag which had a similar texture to human skin, and even had human hair implanted in it.

In 98% of cases, the lure worked, but funding started to dry up (although [[Neil Thompson|Neil]] may have claimed this due to gambling debts). [[Neil Thompson|Neil]] was descended from the 19th century physician [John Snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Snow) and had a Victorian syringe that John had once owned. In order to pay his debts and raise funds, [[Neil Thompson|Neil]] decided to sell it. The buyer of the syringe is [[Mikaele Salesa]], where he and [[Neil Thompson|Neil]] exchanged the item and payment in the Three Greyhounds pub in Soho.

After the syringe was sold, strange things started happening around the lab. It became unbearably hot, and the mosquitoes stopped buzzing around their cage, instead waiting on the cage itself. Those working in the lab couldn't find a reason for this sudden change in behaviour. When the blood bags were placed in their cages they would swarm with a new ferocity onto the bags. [[George Larson]], one of the lab technicians, removed one of the blood bags and accidentally dropped it. Instead of the heamoglobish, the bag contained human O-negative blood infected with malaria, yellow fever and a number of other diseases. The lab was locked down to be decontaminated.

[[Neil Thompson|Neil]] became enraged, grabbing a fire extinguisher, he charged into the mosquito cages in an attempt to kill them, as though he blamed them for the recent problems at the lab. The mosquitoes swarmed on him, sending him immediately into shock before killing him.

[[Thomas Neill|Thomas]] was given a glowing recommendation by the facility, and he managed to land a lab tech job at King's College, which involved working with students.

# Post-Statement

[[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] was not able to find anything about this statement from the London School of Hygiene and Tropical Medicine. [[Neil Thompson]] (John accidentally calls him Neil Thomas) died on 30th May 2010 in a lab accident. [[Sasha James|Sasha]] obtained a copy of [[Neil Thompson|Neil]]'s death certificate which listed his death as blood loss and death by misadventure.

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] mentions he has been talking to [[Rosie Zampano|Rosie]] about contacting [[Mikaele Salesa]]. Apparently, [[Mikaele Salesa|Salesa]] has not been seen for almost two years. Rumours in the trade about his whereabouts vary from an early retirement, to dodging a jail sentence, or even, "He was shot dead in Colombia for stealing a priceless artefact from a drug lord."

[[Thomas Neill]] died in 2015. Although [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is not aware of his cause of death, his house was full of antibiotics, so it must have been something very unpleasant.

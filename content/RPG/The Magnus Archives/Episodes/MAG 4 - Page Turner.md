# Statement

[[Dominic Swain]] was a theatre technician, running lighting at small theatre companies around London. He had arranged to meet a past flame, [[Katherine Mendes]], on Saturday 10th November 2012 at the Gate Theatre in Notting Hill for a matinee performance of _The Trojan Women_ that she was in.

[[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] arrived early and went to a local Oxfam charity shop to kill time before the performance. There, he found a plain book bound in something like calf leather with the title _[[Ex Altiora]]_ on the spine in faded gold letters. It was written in Latin, with woodcut images of cliffs, mountains, a tower, and an empty night sky. A bookplate in the cover read, "From the library of [[Jurgen Leitner]]." He bought the book for £4 and, discovering he had lost about an hour looking at the book, he left for the show.

During the performance, he smelled ozone but could not pinpoint the source. [[Katherine Mendes|Katherine]] had a look at the book but did not read much as the images triggered her vertigo.

[[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] did some research but found nothing on the book or [[Jurgen Leitner|Leitner]], except a listing on eBay from 2007, an auction on "[[_The Key of Solomon_]] (1863), owned by [[MacGregor Mathers]] and [[Jurgen Leitner]]", which was won for £1,200 by someone called [[Gertrude Robinson|grbookworm1818]].

He tried to get more information on the book from various buyers, but they were not able to tell him anything other than it was 100-150 years old. The last buyer knew [[Jurgen Leitner|Leitner]], saying he was a big name in the literary scene in the 1990s, a Scandinavian recluse who custom-bound books or asked authors to write books for him. He disappeared around 1995, but dealt with a shop called [[Pinhole Books]].

After doing a theatre performance, [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] went for a walk and found himself in Morden, outside [[Pinhole Books]]. He knocked on the door and was greeted by an old woman with a shaved head who sported numerous tattoos of words all over her body. [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] showed her the book and the woman, [[Mary Keay]], returned to the building, followed by [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]]. After walking through a maze of books, they stopped in a room. There were bookshelves all over the walls, an old desk with papers, fishing wires and safety razors, and a painting of an eye. The painting was an incredibly detailed painting, with intricate patterns and symmetry with the caption, "_Grant us the sight that we may not know, grant us the scent that we may not catch, grant us the sound that we may not call,_" which [[Mary Keay|Mary]] said was painted by [[Gerard Keay|Gerard]].

[[Mary Keay|Mary]] announced that it was a long time since she had seen a [[Jurgen Leitner|Leitner]], but that [[Gerard Keay|Gerard]] kept an eye out. She showed [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] her [[Jurgen Leitner|Leitner]], similar in appearance to [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]]'s, but without the title. As she passed it under the shadows and [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] had a closer look, tiny, warped animal bones fell from the book. He looked at his book again and saw that the images were starker with a [[Lichtenberg Figure]] on the previously empty starry night. He fled the building: his childhood friend, [[Michael Crew]], was struck by lightning after [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] forced him to play in the rain. He survived but was permanently scarred by a [[Lichtenberg Figure]].

Later that night, after returning home, there was a knock on his door. [[Gerard Keay]] stood on the threshold, dressed in a dark long leather coat, with dyed black hair, looking like he had not slept. He looked at _Ex Altiora_ and offered to buy it for £5,000. [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] agreed (thinking that he would have given it away but that "wouldn't count") and [[Gerard Keay|Gerard]] left to get the money.

Whilst he was away, [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] researched the Keays, and found a news article from 2008. [[Mary Keay]]'s body had been found. Death was determined to be by an overdose of painkillers but was ruled a murder by the police, due to large pieces of [[Mary Keay|Mary]]'s skin being removed and hung to dry on fishing wire. A picture attached to the news report showed no tattoos and a full head of hair. [[Gerard Keay|Gerard]] was acquitted after an important piece of evidence was deemed inadmissible.

[[Gerard Keay|Gerard]] returned and burned the book after paying [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]], which stopped the ozone smell which had haunted [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] since he bought the book. [[Gerard Keay|Gerard]] said, "My mother doesn't always know what's best for our family." He picked up the scalding hot bin containing the ashes of the book, saying he'd had worse, and left.

# Post-Statement

[[Jonathan Sims]] notes that he thought they had dealt with what remained of the [[Jurgen Leitner|Leitner]] library after the incident in 1994. The library was known for having custom editions of known texts on demonology and the arcane.

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] and [[Sasha James|Sasha]] have looked into mentions of _[[Ex Altiora]]_, but found no records.

[[Gerard Keay]] is unable to be located for a follow-up interview, as he disappeared after his trial. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] notes that [[Pinhole Books]] was closed after 2008, for obvious reasons, so it would not have been there when [[Dominic Swain|Dominic]] visited it.

In the police reports, [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] found that [[Mary Keay|Mary]]'s flesh had been covered in Sanskrit writing, a script similar to that found in her [[Jurgen Leitner|Leitner]] book.

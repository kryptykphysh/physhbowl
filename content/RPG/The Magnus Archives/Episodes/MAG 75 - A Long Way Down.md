# Statement

[[Stephen Walker]]'s brother, [[Grant Walker|Grant]], was always terrified of heights, especially climbing up and down ladders. They shared a house in Jarrow near Newcastle since [[Grant Walker|Grant]] had been fired from Deloitte in January. [[Grant Walker|Grant]] had problems getting a job which started to breed a contempt between the two brothers.

When the two returned to their house after a night out, they realised that neither of them had their keys. After an argument, [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] spotted an open window to [[Grant Walker|Grant]]'s room. [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] borrowed [[Jim Hancock]]'s, his neighbour's, ladder. [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] started to climb, but as he reached the window the ladder slipped and he fell to the ground, breaking his arm. He remembered the event in stark detail.

[[Stephen Walker|Stephen]]'s phone was smashed by the fall and [[Grant Walker|Grant]] did not have his. After knocking on nearby doors for help, he resolved to climb the ladder into the house. The journey was slow and terrifying for him. As he climbed, [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] saw a man on the opposite road watching. The short, young man wore an old grey, faded suit, and had a lightning shaped scar which climbed up his chest onto his neck. Looking at him, [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] felt like he was falling, but it stopped when he looked away. The man watched [[Grant Walker|Grant]] intently.

Finally, painfully, [[Grant Walker|Grant]] got into the window. [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] saw the man walking quickly away and [[Grant Walker|Grant]] opened the door. He retrieved his phone and called an ambulance.

Just before Christmas, [[Grant Walker|Grant]] got a job with Deloitte, he stayed with [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] but started to pay rent. 

[[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] started to plan a holiday to France to coincide with a conference he was to talk at. When [[Grant Walker|Grant]] found out, he also bought plane tickets and made every effort to join in with all [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]]'s activities, much to his brother's annoyance. This tension was made worse when [[Grant Walker|Grant]] was fired for smoking marijuana on company property, leaving [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] paying for the whole holiday for the two of them.

As a form of petty payback, [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] planned for the two of them to go to the top of Tour Montparnasse without letting [[Grant Walker|Grant]] know what it entailed. Tricked into going up, [[Grant Walker|Grant]] sat as far away from the viewing platform as possible. As [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] enjoyed the view, he suddenly felt extremely dizzy. He looked around, and saw the man with the lightning scar looking at him. He looked bored, but when [[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] was helped up by other tourists, the man had disappeared along with [[Grant Walker|Grant]].

He searched for his brother, but did not realise his phone was out of power until it was too late. He charged it and found he had dozens of missed calls and several hundred text messages. The messages that were not scrambled asked where he, or anyone, was, and where the elevators were. He had also sent a photo of the edge of the viewing platform of Tour Montparnasse which did not have barriers and the edge of a ladder could be seen over the edge. Whenever his calls to [[Grant Walker|Grant]] connected, all he could hear was wind.

[[Stephen Walker|Stephen]] hopes that [[Grant Walker|Grant]] is dead, because otherwise he is probably still on that ladder.

# Post-Statement

[[Jonathan Sims]] suspects that the man with the lightning scar is [[Michael Crew]], noting that possession of the books changes the person reading them. Almost as though the book uses the person, rather than the person using the book. This statement reminds him of [[MAG 21 - Freefall|Robert Kelly]].

[[Basira Hussain|Basira]] suddenly shows up. She is pissed off that the police are covering up the [[MAG 73 - Police Lights|Callum Brodie rescue mission]] and the death of officer Altman. She has brought him all of [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude's]] tapes, figuring he might as well have them since the police are certainly not going to solve [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude's]] murder. She quickly leaves again, saying she is done with anything paranormal.

# Statement

[[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] begins his statement by apologising for [[MAG 27 - A Sturdy Lock|the statement his father made]] the previous week, believing him to be lying for attention and to manipulate his son into moving back home. He goes on to explain.

[[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] spent much of his adult life moving in and out of his parents' house. While his mother was pleasant, if eager for him to move on, his father was overly protective due to his history of hallucinations involving doors. [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] was smothered by his father while living at home, as he would desperately try to keep [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] from moving away due to a belief that his son's hallucinations were a warning of great danger.

At present, [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] believes his father secretly thinks he has an undiagnosed psychiatric disorder, despite extensive testing only ever indicating depression. [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]]'s father has been lonely since his wife's passing, and [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] believes his father is pretending to have the same hallucinations of doors as a ploy to make him worry and move back in. He says he visits his father often but needs to move on with his life, and does not appreciate his father's manipulation.

[[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] first saw a door from beyond reality when he was six. He woke one night to the sound of his skipping rope being dragged out of his bedroom. Unafraid, but upset that something was touching his toys, he chased after it. He followed it down the dimly-lit hallway until he reached an open door standing where a blank wall had once been. [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]]'s skipping rope was partway through the door and he began to shake with terror as more and more of it was pulled into the darkness. He picked up the handle and pulled, but the cord went taut as something pulled back and [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] could feel himself slipping. He let go of the rope and watched it disappear into the door before running back to bed. He told his parents, who believed he was lying to cover for losing his toy. The door was gone and the blank wall had returned.

[[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] saw his second door when he was eleven. On his way to and from school, he would pass by an alleyway with a blank space that unnerved him. One day, despite being unable to see it, he knew that the door his rope disappeared through was beneath the concrete in the blank space.There were new marks in the wall which reminded him of a handprint. [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] felt that the door was playfully challenging him to put his fingers in the hollow of the wall in order to open it. His arm tensed as he prepared to accept the challenge until a friend, Luke, called after him. [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] tore himself away from the alleyway and told his parents about the encounter. He received vigorous psychological examination through his adolescence but, other than seeing the doors, there was no indication he was mentally ill.

When he was thirteen, [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] saw the door underneath a railway bridge. It was made of thick metal and bore a chalk message that read, "WARNING: Danger of Death." There was a pounding on the door, and [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] could not tell if what was on the other side was trying to get out, or if it was knocking to be let in.

When he was fifteen, [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] went to his sweetheart Sandra's house to pick her up for their first date. When he rang the doorbell it sounded wrong, like it was echoing through hundreds of empty corridors. He looked at the door to see it did not lead to their house, and heard fast and steady footsteps approaching. [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] turned and ran just as he heard it open behind him.

When [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] was sixteen, he saw the door laying open on the ground as he stumbled home drunk, leading into an angular labyrinth. In his efforts to walk like he was sober he almost did not see it until it was too late. Through vodka-blurred eyes, he saw a shape calmly walking along the vertical hallway.

When he was eighteen, [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] drove his friends to a concert, stopping at a service station along the way for lunch. There was a scream from the stone structure near the parking lot and drag marks leading to the door, none of which his friends noticed.

[[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] last saw the door almost fifteen years afterwards and he almost wept at the sight of it. There was an empty lot a few blocks away from his apartment in Oxford. A week before [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] was to move back into his parents' home, he passed the lot to see a pale yellow door standing freely without a wall or frame to support it. Unlike previous times, [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] sensed no playfulness or malicious joy from it, only hunger and cold anger. It seemed to scream at him to open it and he couldn't resist the call and walk away, insinuating that he went into the halls and was let out.

At present, [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] apologises for getting so deep into his own issues. He says that the depth of meaning behind the door is why his father is using it as a focal point in his own story, and again insists he is only pretending to see it.

# Post-Statement

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] expresses frustration over how much he worked to find [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]]'s statement when he first recorded his father's statement. Instead, he found the statement when he was drawn to rearrange an old filing cabinet and found the statement lost behind it. He says he misses the old days where he could attribute the statements to drug abuse or mental illness. He now knows that almost all of the statements were true, and [[Jonathan Sims|John]] laments [[Paul McKenzie]]'s suffering while also commenting on his own knowledge of doors. The recorder clicks off.

The recorder clicks back on to [[Jonathan Sims|John]] pounding on a door. [[The Distortion|Helen]] opens the door and [[Jonathan Sims|John]] asks her why she never told him about [[Marcus MacKenzie]] or his father. [[The Distortion|Helen]] says she was pursuing the pair long before she was even [[The Distortion|Michael]], and laments missing the chance to eat [[Paul MacKenzie|Paul]] before his natural death. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] wants to know what happened to [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] as he can no longer be reached, and [[The Distortion|Helen]] alludes to eating him some months prior. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is appalled and asks why, but [[The Distortion|Helen]] doesn't have an answer for him other than that her style of terror has shifted. [[The Distortion|Helen]] compares her own method of feeding to [[Jonathan Sims|John]]'s and advises him to embrace his needs.

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] wants to know why [[The Distortion|Helen]] was trying to lure [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] into [[Hill Top Road]]. She says there is something wrong with [[Hill Top Road]], calling it a, "Strange [[Gap in Reality|scar on reality]]," integral to whatever [[The Web]] is doing. [[The Distortion|Helen]] wanted to see what would happen if she lured [[Marcus MacKenzie|Marcus]] inside, but did not have the same skill for manipulation as [[The Web]] and was unsuccessful. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] asks if she was compelled by [[The Web]] to do that, to which [[The Distortion|Helen]] says she doesn't think so, but due to the subtlety with which [[The Web]] acts is not completely sure. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] becomes increasingly agitated and asks if [[The Web]] can control an [[Avatar]] of a different power and compel them to feed against their will, but [[The Distortion|Helen]] laughs. She mockingly asks if he's really sure didn't want to feed, and continues to laugh as her door closes. The recorder clicks off.

When it clicks back on again, [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is in a room with [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]], [[Melanie King|Melanie]], and [[Basira Hussain|Basira]]. [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] sent them the tape with [[Jonathan Sims|John]]'s coffee shop victim and they demand an explanation. [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] wants to know how many times [[Jonathan Sims|John]] has fed. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] counts 4 then corrects to 5 after including [[Floyd Matharu]], his victim on the boat to [[Ny-Ålesund]]. He says that the woman who didn't give her name in the statement to [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]], [[Jess Terrell]], was his fourth victim.

The first was a supermarket cleaner who was lost for a week in an endless warehouse. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] claims to have only gone in for shopping before forcing the worker to tell his story. The second was a woman who woke up in a fresh grave every year on her birthday. After being stabbed by [[Melanie King|Melanie]], [[Jonathan Sims|John]] went out to walk in the streets. He thought he was trying to clear his head but [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] accuses him of hunting, to which [[Jonathan Sims|John]] agrees. The third was a lonesome man who sought love from increasingly dark places that wept maggots while [[Jonathan Sims|John]] compelled him to speak.

[[Basira Hussain|Basira]] stops [[Jonathan Sims|John]] before he can say any more. [[Melanie King|Melanie]] asks why [[Jonathan Sims|John]] did not record the statements, to which [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] suggests he was ashamed, but [[Jonathan Sims|John]] says he lets the tape recorder start on its own rather tape each individual statement and assumes they will record whatever is important. [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] says [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is a monster hurting innocent people. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] compares his actions to [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]]'s, but [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] insists her situation was different and points out that [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] has been vigilant in resisting her hunger since realising her need. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] says he does not know if he can control his hunger and suggests [[The Web]] might be controlling him. [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] says that to find out if he is being controlled they need to find [[Annabelle Cane]], and [[Jonathan Sims|John]] mentions her connection to [[Hill Top Road]].

[[Basira Hussain|Basira]] decides they will go to [[Hill Top Road]] immediately, which is met by resistance from [[Melanie King|Melanie]], [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]], and [[Jonathan Sims|John]]. [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] resolves to go alone if the others will not go with her and leaves. Defeated, [[Jonathan Sims|John]] concludes that they're going with her as [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] and [[Melanie King|Melanie]] leave the room. The recorder clicks off.

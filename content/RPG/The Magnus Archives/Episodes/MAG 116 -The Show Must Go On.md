# Pre-Statement

[[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] has called the staff (absent [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] and [[Melanie King|Melanie]]) together to discuss their plans to disrupt [[The Unknowing]]. Despite the plan's simplicity, he believes it has a reasonable chance of succeeding. [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] questions if the plan is fully-formed, as he believes they have only gotten as far as sneaking into the museum and detonating the explosives when [[Circus of the Other|The Circus]] is distracted. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] believes the [[Rituals|ritual]] will be vulnerable once it has begun, while [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] and [[Alice Daisy Tonner|Daisy]] confirm that the scheme need not be overly convoluted.

[[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] warns that they may all end up confronting [[The Stranger]] directly and thinks it would be best to prepare them for what such an encounter may entail. He offers to play one of [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude's]] tapes despite the group's exasperation.

# Statement

[[Abraham Janssen]] reflects on the mania-inducing event that has left him mentally-scarred, but concedes there were others who escaped said event with graver wounds. He curses the name of [[Wolfgang von Kempelen]] and his invention, [the Mechanical Turk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_Turk).

[[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]], a formidable chess player, learned of the Turk, a chess-playing automaton, at its inception. [[Wolfgang von Kempelen|Kempelen]] asked him to be the Turk's puppetmaster, revealing it was, in fact, controlled by a human operator deep within its base. [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] was delighted to assist and disregarded the deception, as the Turk's game-playing movements were a marvel in and of itself, and says it would be a disservice to dismiss [[Wolfgang von Kempelen|Kempelen]]'s ingenuity and the Turk's clockwork mechanisms.

Despite his intellect, [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] found [[Wolfgang von Kempelen|Kempelen]] an odd acquaintance and treated his manner with some reproach, as they never conversed together in their mother tongues. He sensed secret ambitions within [[Wolfgang von Kempelen|Kempelen]] regarding his machines, but could never deduce them. When he finally viewed the Turk, [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] was inflicted with terror and found it grotesque. The dark, still face with unmoving painted eyes filled him with revulsion, and he felt as if he was being devoured whenever he climbed into the belly of the device to operate it.

They exhibited the Turk and were met with unqualified praise and their ruse left unsuspected. Despite a year of travel with the device, [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] could never quiet the anxiety he felt whenever he viewed the automaton, nor the strange and intricate dreams he had of it. 

[[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] eventually returned to London for business, parting ways with [[Wolfgang von Kempelen|Kempelen]], and terrified of his next proposed invention: a machine he insisted would be capable of mastering human speech.

About fifteen years later, [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] received an invitation from Wolfgang to attend a grand performance at the newly completed Court Theatre of Buda. He gives in to [[Wolfgang von Kempelen|Kempelen]]'s pleading, despite deep apprehension for his machines, and agrees to attend.

Despite his attempts to locate [[Wolfgang von Kempelen|Wolfgang]], [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] is only able to learn more of the Court Theatre itself, discovering it was once a Carmelite monastery converted into a theatre under [[Wolfgang von Kempelen|Kempelen]]'s supervision, placing the stage where the high altar once stood.

When [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] and other attendees entered the theatre for the performance, [[Abraham Janssen|Abraham]] realized every usher was, in fact, an automaton, each resembling the Turk. Dressed as footmen, the figures ushered everyone within with jerky, unnatural motions. He attempted to converse with the other guests, but was suddenly restrained by one machine with incredible strength which guided him to a balcony seat.

[[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] and the other guests, at this point, bewildered and frightened, found a small metal cage containing a tiny mechanical bird before each seat. Once every seat was filled, all eyes fell upon a figure on stage: the Turk, draped in a hairy, coarse skin instead of a coat. 

With a single nod of the Turk, every bird began a discordant, horrendous wail. As the birds' screams intensified, the Turk raised a sword above its head and slammed it down, shattering a chessboard placed on the table before it. Blood began pooling onto the stage as the Turk placed a new device resembling a throat connected to a bellows on the table. At this point, the Turk stood and began to dance despite not having any legs, while the bellows began to pump and the throat, Wolfgang's speech machine, shrieked Hungarian at the audience. At this moment, [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] experiences complete mania, describing a moment of absolute nothingness, the vanishing of his limbs and crying sweet sherry.

The moment is ended at the sound of a battle cry, as [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] witnesses a company of soldiers burst into the theatre. The soldiers wept despite empty eye sockets. They had ropes tied around their necks which was handled by their captain, who cut down any intervening clockwork automaton with his sword. The blind soldiers carried forth a cannon which was loaded and fired at the Turk, destroying it and restoring the theatre to reality.

The soldiers departed, ignoring the carnage and pleading of the wounded. [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]] fled the scene.

# Final Comments

[[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] remarks on the resilience of [[The Stranger]]'s ritual compared to other [[The Entities|Entities']], and while easy to delay, deems full disruption impossible. Reflecting upon [[The Slaughter's]] [[Avatar]]'s straight-forward disruption of a fully-realized [[Rituals|ritual]], [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] hypothesizes that the closer [[The Stranger]]'s ritual is to completion, the more vulnerable it is to physical interruption. She concludes such a method would be a viable Plan B and intends to inquire about [[Adelard Dekker|acquiring explosives]].

She surmises that the hairy skin seen on the Turk was the [[Gorilla Skin]], and confirms the skin is still being used as a focusing item for the [[Rituals|ritual]]. Despite incurring direct cannon fire, it was left undamaged and she will need to research a proper method to destroy it. [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]]'s statement at least confirms its location to her.

# Post-Statement

The group discusses [[Abraham Janssen|Janssen]]'s statement and deduce the next ritual attempt will be different in performance, but consistent in its absurdity and they will not be able to trust their senses as [[The Stranger]] will attempt to throw everything into doubt.

[[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] agrees with [[Jonathan Sims|John]] that [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] should stay behind at the Archives with Melanie instead of attempting to disrupt the ritual despite [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]]'s fervent protestations. [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] heavily advises against allowing [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] to come, and [[Jonathan Sims|John]] says that he will take it into consideration.

[[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] exits and Basira asks the remaining staff if he bought their ruse, but [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] interjects that they can discuss it later.

Later, [[Jonathan Sims|John]] informs [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] that despite [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] not wanting [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] attending the mission, [[Jonathan Sims|John]] wants him to go. He questions [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]]'s stability, to which [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] responds that he will keep it together for the success of the mission, but when it all goes awry, to stay out of his way.

# Statement

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] finds a man he doesn't recognize in the Archives and tries to usher him away from a restricted area, but his reactions prompt [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] to ask if [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] Lukas sent him. The man introduces himself as [[Simon Fairchild]] and explains that he is here to answer any questions [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] might have, as repayment for losing a bet to [[Peter Lukas|Peter]]. When confronted, [[Simon Fairchild|Fairchild]] cannot give any clear answers about [[The Extinction]], opting instead to insist that [[The Entities]] function according to "emotional logic", and that the new Power will be born when it feels like it.

[[Simon Fairchild|Simon]] proceeds to present [[Peter Lukas|Peter]]'s point of view, according to which [[The Extinction]] is bound to emerge very soon, and that its birth has potential to be as devastating as a full manifestation of an ordinary Power. [[Simon Fairchild|Fairchild]] disagrees with the latter point, because his patron has gifted him an extremely prolonged existence, which helps put matters into perspective.

He talks about the last [[Rituals|ritual]] he attempted: The [[Awful Deep]] in 1853, which was meant to cash in on the aquarium mania and involved a special diving bell full of sacrifices. [[Simon Fairchild|Simon]] believes that the ritual failed because it was a bad idea, but interference from a [[The Hunt|Hunter]] did not help either. He begrudges that the world feels too small nowadays.

When asked directly about [[The Entities]], [[Simon Fairchild|Fairchild]] doubts their ability to do anything, and their servants are simply trying their best to interpret vague sensations. He compares it to being deaf and trying to replicate music heard in a dream, while having to invent the concept of a musical instrument from scratch. [[Simon Fairchild|Simon]] concludes by saying that [[Rituals]] might as well be some cosmic joke, eternally unachievable.

He does not say much regarding his own history - only that he has been [[Simon Fairchild]] for 80-90 years and came into money by eliminating the previous Mr. Fairchild in a way that included a very long fall and 1930s tax practices. His servitude to the [[The Vast|Falling Titan]] began during his apprenticeship under Tintoretto, who left young [[Simon Fairchild|Simon]] to draw the backgrounds. One day, when he lost his footing, instead of falling to the floor, he fell into the sky.

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] concludes the meeting by asking if [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] was honest with him about his promise to protect the others, to which [[Simon Fairchild|Simon]] replies that [[Peter Lukas|Lukas]] may have oversold his abilities but is trying his best. He theorises that his plan may include something in the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]], which would require someone touched by [[The Eye|Beholding]] to use it. [[Simon Fairchild|Fairchild]] considers it a calculated gamble, but remains unconcerned about the impending apocalypse.

# Post-Statement

Upon seeing [[Simon Fairchild]] leave, [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] confronts [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] about his shady dealings with spooky old men. She seems to know that it was [[Simon Fairchild]]. [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] explains that it all comes back to [[Peter Lukas|Lukas']] [[The Extinction|Extinction]] prevention plan, while [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] doubts that they would do the world a favour out of the goodness of their hearts. She shares with [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] the fact that everyone has listened to the tapes he made for [[Jonathan Sims|John]]. Listening to [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] talk about the need to prevent the apocalypse, she realises that he does not intend to come out of this alive, but nevertheless decides to trust him on this - and to keep this exchange from [[Jonathan Sims|John]].

# Trivia

- Tintoretto was a famous Venetian painter from the 16th century - meaning that [[Simon Fairchild]] is around 500 years old.
    - The painting [[Simon Fairchild|Simon]] fell in while painting is likely "Il Paradiso" by Tintoretto, which remains one of the largest canvas paintings in the world till this day - a literal "big picture".

# Pre-Statement

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] confronts [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] in prison about [[The Extinction]]. He asks if [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] is telling the truth about anything, and [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] tells him that everything [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] has told him is true. [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] is legitimately trying to stop the end of the world, but [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] is not helping, because his relationship with the apocalypse is complicated. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] does not know about [[The Extinction]]. [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] has recently had to come to terms with [[The Extinction]] being real and he has no real control over what [[Jonathan Sims|John]] knows anymore. [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] thinks that [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] wants him to join [[The Lonely]]. [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] thinks the decision of joining [[The Lonely]] or not is too important to interfere with. [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] then leaves with a decision to make.

# Statement

[[Robert Smirke]] believes that his hour of death is approaching. He sees his past hubris in [[Jonah Magnus]]. He will not go easy to the grave without giving [[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] one last warning. He believes what they built in Millbank should be left alone. He has lived a long life for someone who has crossed paths with the [[The Entities|Dread Powers]], but he now believes that he might have wasted his life's efforts. He has begun to lose faith in the idea that there is any possibility of achieving balance between [[The Entities]].

Out of all those who [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] has brought into his confidence, it is only him and [[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] who have not fallen to any power, assuming that [[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] has not taken the path of [[The Eye]] that has called them both. [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] assumes [[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] can recall what happened with the Reform Club, but there have been other experiments below the streets of London that [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] covered up with churches. When [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] was younger, he would have dreams of strange, far-off places. He believed that they were the entities themselves, in their truest form. He believed that with the right space and architecture, the powers could be harnessed and channeled, but he now believes that it was his own hubris.

So many of those that [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] brought into his confidence had abandoned them, casting about for rituals that he had helped to create. In his discussion with [[Maxwell Rayner|Mr. Rayner]], he perhaps extrapolated too much from [[Maxwell Rayner|Rayner's]] words of a grand ritual of darkness. [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] thought that [[The Dark]] was simply one of the powers, so it stands to reason that all of them should have a ritual. He thinks maybe they did before he put them down on paper. He shudders to think of how Lukas, Scott, and others may use them.

[[Robert Smirke|Robert]] should have realized from their first discussion of [[The Flesh]] that the powers could never be truly balanced, when new fears can emerge. [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] knows that [[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] thinks [[The Flesh]] has always been there, tucked away until its recent emergence, but to grant the existence of lesser power would throw things into confusion. Would you separate insects, dirt, and disease from [[The Corruption]]? [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] believes that the only explanation is that it is a new power.

[[Robert Smirke|Robert]] has been dreaming the same dream for months now. He is in an empty house and the sky is dark until it blinks, and then he wakes up. [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] knows what this dream means and he warns [[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] again to abandon any ambitions of wearing the Watcher's Crown. [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] wonders how the powers came to be in the first place, whether they were eternal or created by fear in some grand accident.

[[Robert Smirke|Robert]] is being watched by people everywhere he goes. He tried to dismiss it, but it got too much when his driver turned his head all the way around to watch [[Robert Smirke|Robert]], looking like a sculptor had placed his head on his body in reverse. He is choosing to believe that the manifestations are unintentional and that [[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] has not sought to implore a dark patron to end the life of an old man. He urges [[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] to not pursue the [[The Entities|Dread Powers]].

Ehe night before, [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] was woken up by a noise in the drawing room. The noise came again and he called out to his daughter, [[Laura Smirke|Laura]], to ask if she had woken up in the night, but got no reply. He lit a candle and crept towards the drawing room. In the dim glow of his candle, he could see a figure in the corner, wearing a long nightdress that, at a glance, made it seem like [[Laura Smirke|Laura]]. He asked what she was doing, but she just continued staring into the corner. She began to slowly turn before his candle went out. When he relit it, he could see [[Laura Smirke|Laura]]'s face inches from his own. Her eyes stared into his, taking up half of her face or more, bulging out of their sockets. [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] screamed just once, but [[Laura Smirke|Laura]] gave no reply. He wanted to run, but he could do nothing but freeze and stare back at her. After a while, the candle went out and he stood there until morning, only to find [[Laura Smirke|Laura]] gone. [[Laura Smirke|Laura]] claims to have no knowledge of this, having no memory of leaving her chamber. [[The Eye]] has marked [[Robert Smirke|Robert]], and he hopes that his death may be swift. He once again implores [[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] to give up on his research of the [[The Entities|Dread Powers]]. The letter cuts off.

# Post-Statement

[[Robert Smirke]] was found collapsed in his study that evening, dead of apoplexy. [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] wonders what the letter means, whether there is a precedent for [[The Extinction]] or not. He calls out to [[Peter Lukas|Peter]], before deciding that maybe he was at a party after all. He thinks that [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] was obviously wrong about balancing the powers, as it would be impossible with so much variation. He does not know what [[Robert Smirke|Robert]] was talking about when he mentioned Millbank. [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] said something about the tunnels under the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]], but [[Jonathan Sims|John]] checked them pretty thoroughly. [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] does not know what [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] is planning, but he guesses that it might involve something beneath the Institute. He hopes that by the time [[Jonathan Sims|John]] gets these tapes, he will have something more concrete. He wishes [[Jonathan Sims|John]] good luck and tells him to stay safe.

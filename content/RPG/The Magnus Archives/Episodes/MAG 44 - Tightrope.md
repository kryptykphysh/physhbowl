# Statement

[[Yuri Utkin]] loved the circus when he was a boy, but lived in a poor village where the circus did not tour. Every year, his father would take him and his brother [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] on a trip to Morshansk to see the government-run circus. [[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]] loved watching the clowns, but felt uncomfortable watching the acrobats after witnessing a childhood friend, Piotr, fall and break his leg very badly.

In early November 1952, a strange new circus came to the village, calling itself [[The Circus of the Other|другой Цирк]] (English: Another Circus/The Different Circus). Although [[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]]'s father was initially suspicious, he agreed to take his two children to the circus after he realised much of the village would be attending. As they approached the gate, they met a woman in a leotard that spoke strangely and who was unaffected by the cold. A [[Calliope|calliope]] organ could be heard playing and [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] ran past the gate in excitement.

[[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]] heard a tiger roar as he entered the circus, and he ran from his father to find it. After locating the tiger he stared at it intensely, noticing that its eyes were glassy and its movements stiff. The tiger roared without opening its mouth and [[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]] fell back, only to be picked up by [[Breekon & Hope|two huge men in overalls]]. In crude Russian that seemed to flow back and forth between them, they told him that the tiger, "Wasn't finished" and returned him to his father.

[[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]]'s father asked the ringmaster where they might find [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] and was told that, while the staff would be instructed to keep an eye out for the boy, [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] had likely wandered off in excitement and would return when the show started. The ringmaster assured the worried man that the circus had yet to lose a child, but as he spoke [[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]] was reminded of the strange tiger and resolved to find [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] himself. During his search he came across a smaller tent with a sign written in English, likely reading "Freakshow." There, he saw many impossibly deformed people standing silently in cages. At the end of the tent, he found a large, rough sack tied with coarse rope. As the [[Calliope]] started to play to signal the start of the show, the bag began to writhe and [[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]] fled to the main tent.

Inside the tent, he found a crowd of Algasovo residents, including his father, cheering wildly as two clowns brutally attacked each other. Looking up, he saw [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] slowing walking the tightrope while crying, terrified. [[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]] was sure he would fall with every step, yet was powerless to do anything but watch. [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] successfully reached the end of the tightrope and disappeared from sight. A moment later, [[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]]'s father appeared with [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] at his side and they left the circus, shaken.

The circus had gone the next morning. For many years [[Yuri Utkin|Yuri]] thought the events of that day were a terrible dream, but he spoke to [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] about it as an adult. His brother could remember the day and going through the gate, but nothing else. Each November he would have nightmares of being tied up in a rough sack and would wake up screaming.

# Final Comments

[[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] remarks that the two brothers were lucky to have only suffered significant mental trauma after their experience, as [[Gregor Orsinov's]] troupe had been particularly dangerous at that time and it was remarkable that the village did not suffer any casualties following their encounter. The troupe had become more tame after [[Nikolai Denikin]] left in the 70s and such a tame encounter would have been more likely at that time. She is interested in knowing what [[Ivan Utkin|Ivan]] would have been like, but he passed away in 1984.

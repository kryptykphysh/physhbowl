# Statement
There is a community centre near [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]]'s house that in the past few years has started offering adult education programs. These included things like a cheese-making course and a fire-eating workshop. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] generally has her days to herself, so she signed up for a lot of the programs. The workshop schedule started listing a bi-weekly sculpting class and [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] signed up for it.

The class was small because of the high material cost and early time, so there were only four other people: Mary, Bill, [[Ray Quinton]], who was teaching the class, and a fourth man who [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] did not know. He introduced himself as, "[[Gabriel]]," and was a short, squat man with knobbly bare arms that almost reached down to his knees. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] was not one to judge on appearances, so it was not his ugliness that set her against him. There was something about how he moved like he was always too close and in your personal space, until you looked again and then he was not. When [[Gabriel]] came up to [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] without warning and introduced himself, [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] had to stop herself from obviously recoiling.

The first lessons were all very basic. The class just listened and watched [[Ray Quinton|Ray]]'s demonstrations, except for [[Gabriel]]. He ignored their teacher and focused on the clay in front of him. His thick fingers were able to twist and shape the clay with ease. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] always found clay a difficult, heavy material to work with, but looking over at [[Gabriel]] made it seem like the clay was alive in his hands. During one of their earlier projects, they were supposed to make a fish, nice and simple. [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] had even made some of the armatures already. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] still struggled, and Mary and Bill had a good go at it, but [[Gabriel]] made something that was not a fish. It almost started out as a fish, but it kept going and looping through itself. After a half an hour, [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] had almost forgotten about her work and just watched him make his serpentine structure. Every time [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] came over, he would just nod to himself and trade a few words with [[Gabriel]], as if whatever he was making was in any way similar to the assignment.

At one part, [[Gabriel]] saw [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] staring and walked over to her with the sculpture in hand and held it towards her face. He asked her for help because he was in a bit of a creative block, but [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] could only think that he had the palest eyes she had ever seen. The clay in his hands started to move, coiling around through its face and flailed its long, distorted fins. It made a noise that sounded like a scream heard through water and stretched out to [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]]'s mouth, which was hanging open in horror. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] screamed in horror and fell backwards, and is sure that if she had not, the thing would have dived down her throat. Everyone else rushed over, but she could only watch as [[Gabriel]] made his way back to his bench. He said "What an excellent idea. A very promising direction indeed."

After that, [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] quit the class, emailing [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] about it and making references to inappropriate behaviour from another student. She continued on with her life and did not give [[Gabriel]] another thought until she walked into her salsa class the following Thursday to see the room set up for sculpture. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] was taken back by the very significant scheduling issue until she checked the timetable of all of the classes to find that it seemed like sculpting had always been on Thursdays. [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] made a big show of her coming back, so she felt like she could not really leave. Bill gave a slightly pained smile, but when [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] asked if he was alright, he shrugged and said he could not complain. There was no sign of Mary, and they still have not found her.

[[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] started to work. [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] told the class that their lesson was on faces. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] put her hand up to say that faces seemed a bit advanced for where they were in the course, but [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] only responded by saying that they were a lot more talented than he had thought. He said that the key was faces were twisted, and all you had to do was reach into the deepest part of yourself and put that twisted on the outside of the clay, and as soon as you can scream you will have your own face staring back at you. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] asked him to clarify, but [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] just repeated himself: soon as you can scream. He then glanced over to [[Gabriel]], who gave him a big thumbs up, like it was a joke they were playing.

[[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] did her best, and she thinks she made a pretty good stab at it, given how unhelpful the instructions they were given were. The face Bill was working on looked like Mary, but he had made her mouth much much too wide. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] heard a shuffling coming from the other side of the room and knew that it was [[Gabriel]]. He coughed gently, and [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] thought it would have been rude not to look. The structure he held made her eyes hurt. Thin, sharp lines angled off of each other but they never seemed to actually connect with each other. The sculpture shifted, like the other one [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] saw. She felt something jabbing at her skull like a migraine. Finally, the lines seemed to resolve into the clear shape of a door. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] asked if it was supposed to be a face, and [[Gabriel]] told her yes, that it was a good friend of his. She asked who, and he said that they did not have a name, that having one would only confuse them. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]]'s head was still pounding. [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] was still nodding and smiling and Bill was shoveling fistfuls of the clay Mary into his mouth. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] does not remember any more of that lesson.

The last lesson was on Monday. It was not watercolours and never has been according to the schedule. [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] was in front, singing in a high tenor. When [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] asked what they were doing, he just sang louder. Bill was there, but he was made of clay, his limbs being worked on by [[Gabriel]]. As soon as [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] entered, [[Gabriel]] ran up to her, grabbing her hand and thanking her for being the best assistant he could have ever had and that he had improved so much because of her. As he was saying that, his face shifted, pushing further and further from where it should have been. Behind his teeth and at the edges of his eyes, [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] saw the dull red of clay shifting. His smile kept getting wider and where his fingers touched the back of her hand, [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] could feel his fingerprints turning. The marks it left would not come off afterward. She ran after that.

[[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] is convinced that it was not real, as she has seen [[Ray Quinton|Ray]] and Bill around. They do not like to make eye contact and Bill cries at church, but they are still there. She got a letter a week ago from [[Gabriel]], inviting her to assist him in a new place called [Sannikov Land]. [[Deborah Madaki|Deborah]] looked it up and it does not exist. She does not think she should go, and decides that she will not go.

# Post-Statement

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] notes that [[Gabriel]] is the "Worker in Clay" from [[MAG 101: Another Twist]], who took part in [[The Great Twisting]], which [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] stopped at the cost of a single life. He still considers this great sacrifice to be unacceptable, and sad. He looked at [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude's]] notebook (found among the plastic explosives in [[MAG 113 - Breathing Room]]), but does not understand the contents: they are not encoded but contain largely simple numbers and sentence fragments that mean little to anyone else. From staring at the notebook, he found this statement but considers it of little use beyond as background and clues to how [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] formulated her counter-rituals.

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] is worried about [[Melanie King|Melanie]], who refuses to speak to him, and hopes that he was right to remove the bullet from her leg and was not, "Too late again". He mentions that a tape recorder was present when he sat down and that whatever controls them, they are there for him and have proven useful. There were no tape recordings made during his absence.

He followed up on [[Deborah Madaki]], who did not go to [[Sannikov Land]]. This could have been because she decided not to, or because shortly after this statement was given, the body of one [[Mary Randall]] was found in her basement, and [[Deborah Madaki|Madaki]] has been incarcerated in Eastwood Park Prison ever since. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] found no information about [[Mary Randall|Randall's]] body but can imagine what a sculptor's apprentice might be capable of--even an unwilling one.

[[Martin Blackwood]] hears a [[Tape Recorders|tape recorder]] click on, and begins to talk to it like a pet, remarking that it is back because [[Jonathan Sims|John]] is back, and that he and the recorder both missed him. [[Peter Lukas]] appears and comments that this is not a great sign; he and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] argue over the necessity of total isolation for unspecified power. If they do not, "Pull this off," requiring [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] as, "The only one who could possibly balance between the two," it will be over for everyone, including [[Jonathan Sims|John]]. [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] continues his cheerful petty manipulation: he insults [[Elias Bouchard]] for his lack of foresight in believing that he could complete [[The Watcher's Crown]] and thus eliminate all other problems, and [[Jonathan Sims|John]] for his poor archival practices. He has found some old statements by [[Adelard Dekker]], which should provide context for [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]].

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] says that once everything is over, he will tell [[Jonathan Sims|John]] everything; [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] replies that at that point, he will not want to, but everyone will be safe. He tries to look at [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]]'s employee schedule but is extremely incompetent with the computer, and remarks that he has an assistant for this.

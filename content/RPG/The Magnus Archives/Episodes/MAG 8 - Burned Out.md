# Statement

[[Ivo Lensik]] works in construction, having inherited his father's family business. His father and great-uncle had schizophrenia, his father's disease developing around the time of his divorce. He would spend many hours in his study drawing and analysing [fractals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractal). He told his son that someone was following him, trying to stop his research and that [[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]] would know him when they met because "all the bones are in his hands". Shortly afterwards [[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]]'s father apparently committed suicide, though the tools that he used were never found and the coroner noted there was a look of fear on his face.

In mid-November 2006, [[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]] started work on a house on [[Hill Top Road]] as a second job in order to earn money for his upcoming wedding to his fiancée, Sam. On his first day there was a knock on the door; he answered it to reveal a clean-shaven man with shaggy chestnut hair and a tan coat who claimed to be [[Raymond Fielding]], the owner of the house. [[Raymond Fielding|Raymond]] produced the deed to the house as proof.

[[Raymond Fielding|Raymond]] came into the house and stared at the huge tree in the garden before abruptly disappearing, leaving behind a scorched wooden floor and a smell of burning hair. The smell disappeared as quickly as it arrived. [[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]] slipped on the floor whilst cleaning the mark and fell unconscious for a few seconds. When he awoke, he called an ambulance and was taken to the John Radcliffe Hospital, where he was kept for observation overnight. When telling his doctor of his story (who confirmed the hallucination was not due to early schizophrenia), he noticed another nurse, later revealed to be [[Anna Kasuma]], a Malaysian woman in her 50s, listening into the conversation.

Before he left the hospital, [[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]] spoke to [[Anna Kasuma|Anna]], who told him about the story of the house on [[Hill Top Road]]. [[Raymond Fielding]], a devout churchgoer, set up the house in the 1960s as a half-way house for teenage runaways and those with mental problems, supported by the local Catholic church. [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]] appeared at some point, an 11-year-old girl with mousy pigtails who some claimed was [[Raymond Fielding|Raymond]]'s daughter. After she arrived, the children from the house stopped being a bother to the rest of the town and slowly the occupants of the house seemed to dwindle, [[Raymond Fielding|Raymond]] explaining that they had moved on. Eventually, it was just [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]], aged 18 or 19 by this time, and [[Raymond Fielding|Raymond]]. Then [[Raymond Fielding|Raymond]] disappeared and local pets started to disappear; then in 1974 5-year-old [[Henry White]] went missing. A week later, when the house at [[Hill Top Road]] burned down, the locals were not exactly quick to call the fire brigade. [[Agnes Montague|Agnes']] remains were not found but [[Raymond Fielding|Raymond]]'s were, although he was missing his right hand.

A few days later [[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]] returned to work at [[Hill Top Road]], careful to only be there when others were there. However, one evening he stayed late accidentally. When he realised this, he felt suddenly hot with a heat that radiated from his bones and he could not scream. There came a knock at the door, one [[Edwin Burroughs]], a Catholic priest who the nurse [[Anna Kasuma]] had asked to visit. Explaining he was an exorcist, he said he would do what he could.

[[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]] went into the garden to give [[Edwin Burroughs|Edwin]] some space. There, he was overwhelmed with a hatred for the ominous tree. He struck it with a crowbar and blood spilled from it. He wrapped a chain around the tree and uprooted it with his car. In the hole where the tree was, he found an ornate wooden box with intricate patterns on it, about 6 inches square. Inside was a green apple that looked freshly picked. As he removed it from the box, however, it began to rot and thousands of spiders spilled forth from it. When he dropped the apple, it burst into dust and vermin. He destroyed the box and threw it into a skip.

[[Edwin Burroughs|Edwin]] said he had done what he could and gave [[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]] his business card. [[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]] finished up a week later without further incident.

# Post-Statement

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] did not find any dates about when the house burned down but did find that [[Walter Fielding]] purchased it in 1891, passed it to [[Alfred Fielding]] in 1923, then [[Raymond Fielding|Raymond]] in 1957. There are no records of it being a half-way house, although this could be due to poor record-keeping of the Catholic diocese.

Other residents of [[Hill Top Road]] corroborated [[Anna Kasuma|Mrs Kasuma's]] story about the history of the house. Although [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] interviewed her, she had nothing new to add to the statement.

[[Raymond Fielding|Raymond]] was reported as having died in a house fire; his obituary lauded his work with troubled youths.

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] found a report that may or may not be related: [[Agnes Montague]], 26, was found dead in her flat in Sheffield on 23rd November 2006, the same day [[Ivo Lensik|Ivo]] destroyed the tree. She hanged herself and had a severed human hand tied by a chain to her waist, which appears to have died the same time she did. Obviously, her age does not match up with the [[Agnes Montague|Agnes]] from [[Hill Top Road]].

There have been no further manifestations at [[Hill Top Road]].

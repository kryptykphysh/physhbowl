# Statement

[[Luca Moretti]] says there is no glory in war, only fear, and he tried to not take any lives during battle when the time came to it. He was not a supporter of Mussolini; [[Luca Moretti|Luca]] supported the King.

He and his unit had been trained for mountain combat, but were regularly sent against tanks in open plains. After the King signed the Armistice with the Allies, the Germans [[Luca Moretti|Luca]] had fought with turned against them, eventually imprisoning him and his team in a POW camp. After the war, the division of Italy was messy and divided, and [[Luca Moretti|Luca]] hardly knew the state of his government. 

When he returned to his hometown of Teramo, he found his Alpini comrades preparing for a trek into the mountains of the Appennino Centrale. They explained they were going to track down deserters who had been attacking locals. Antonio Cannavaro, one of his oldest friends, explained that the deserters had 'gone rotten': "sono andanti marcio". [[Luca Moretti|Luca]] agreed to go with them.

[[Luca Moretti|Luca]]'s father fought in the Great War, and when he got drunk would talk about the 'wild deserters' who would run into no-man's land, digging dens into the soil. They would emerge at night, scavenging what they could, and taking corpses down to consume.

The troupe headed into the mountains and found a cabin. In front of it was a woman fixing a thick woollen shirt. She had a bullet hole in her throat and white eyes. She stood up and started walking towards the group. Antonio screamed and charged her down before the whole unit beat the woman until she stopped moving.

There was a sudden crack of a gunfire, everyone scattered except Alfredo, who had been shot through the skull. He collapsed after 5 minutes and did not get back up. It was then that the group realised they would all die in the mountain.

Over the next few days they roamed the mountain, being picked off one-by-one. Eventually they found a cave, by which time only [[Luca Moretti|Luca]], Antonio, and a younger man remained. The cave stank of their death. Antonio kissed [[Luca Moretti|Luca]] on the forehead and leapt from the cliff to his death. [[Luca Moretti|Luca]] headed into the cave without checking for the fate of the other man.

[[Luca Moretti|Luca]] headed into the smooth cave and slowly the cave changed, until eventually he stumbled upon the corpses of military men wearing uniforms from several eras. As he continued into the cave, the corpses became thicker, and he had to climb over them. They all abruptly opened their eyes to watch [[Luca Moretti|Luca]] climb over them, and they may have started to sing. 

At the end of the cave he found the mud-caked deserter surrounded by blackened bandages and dead rats. The young deserter raised his rifle to shoot [[Luca Moretti|Luca]], but was shot dead by a firing squad.

[[Luca Moretti|Luca]] left the cave and his home and his country and came to [[The Magnus Institute]] to give his statement.

# Post-Statement

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] does not enjoy recording the statements as he finds them extremely draining but has continued to do them even after [[Jonathan Sims|Jonathan]] returned. [[Melanie King|Melanie]] was asking about cases from wars which lead [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] to this statement.

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] asked [[Melanie King|Melanie]] about the [[Breekon & Hope]] depot.

In the middle of talking, [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] asks [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] to pass her a pen, revealing that she has been sitting nearby for several hours without [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] noticing but was not listening to him. She was reading 'Introduction to Alchemy' and explains that, although she is a hostage, she finds the books around the Archives fascinating.

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] asks why she is not trying to escape, to which she asks how his escape attempt is going. She also points out that there may actually be useful information in the library and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] agrees with her.

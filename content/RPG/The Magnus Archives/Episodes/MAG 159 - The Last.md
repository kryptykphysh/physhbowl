# Pre-Statement

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] has entered [[The Lonely]], calling out to find [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]]. [[Peter Lukas|Peter]]'s voice fades in and out, taunting [[Jonathan Sims|John]] and claiming that [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] does not want to be found.

Eventually, [[Jonathan Sims|John]] begins to taunt [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] back, pointing out that [[The Eye]] and [[The Lonely]] are not so different and that [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] is not powerful enough to force him to leave.

[[Jonathan Sims|John]] finds [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]], who asks why he came for him. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] begs him to leave, but [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] refuses, stating that it "feels right" and "nothing hurts here." [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] states "I really loved you, you know," before disappearing. [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] returns and taunts [[Jonathan Sims|John]] again. [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] tries to convince him to stay, and he pretends to agree before forcing [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] to give him a statement about his life.

# Statement

[[Peter Lukas|Peter]] had four siblings and a deeply faithful mother. His father left when he was young, and [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] slowly lost all his siblings. His mother is alive, though [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] says he cannot remember her face. Two of his sisters left the family. His brother and other sister were sent away young and never returned. [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] acknowledges that his mother may have lied and actually killed them, but he does not believe she was cruel enough. [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] was the favoured son, as he was naturally very lonely and kept to himself.

[[The Lukas Family]] was wealthy, [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] states, but his upbringing was not very normal. His mother believed that friendship was dangerous. As he got older, he would periodically steal money from his mother and run away for days at a time. He acknowledges that, looking back, she probably left so much cash in her purse on purpose. He would walk the darkened streets alone in whatever town he ran away to, and would simply revel in the distance he put between him and his family. He hated when he would pass another person, wishing they would disappear; until one day, one of them did when [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] asked him to go away.

When he returned, his mother and a bunch of relatives were waiting. They took him downstairs and showed him their god, presumably [[The Lonely]].

He describes the process of getting the crew together for his expedition to the Arctic, and how much he loved being alone in his cabin, so far from anyone except his crew. Eventually he meets [[Adelard Dekker]], who tells him about [[The Extinction]] -- [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] believes that a world completely devoid of human life would defeat the purpose of [[The Lonely]] and what makes it so special to truly distance yourself from everyone, and hopes that if he can complete his own [[Rituals|ritual]], [[The Extinction]] will be stopped.

[[Peter Lukas|Peter]] invested in an apartment building in London, hoping to use it to summon [[The Lonely]] through a ritual he dubs the [[Silence]]. He creates the building to be extremely lonely; the first floors are empty, so the only people living there can only see people on the streets from a distance. There is an abundance of false doors, so that knocking on your neighbour's door will often get no answer. The rooms are small, with no room for more than a single bed and a small couch, and the kitchens cannot do much more than microwave. Each room has an office space with no door, so you cannot escape your work. He then rented each room out incredibly cheaply, but only to people who were predisposed to loneliness; recent college graduates who are new to London, recent divorcees, etc. He then planned to wait until their despair reached a maximum, lock them inside their rooms, cut the phone lines and internet, and leave them all to die.

However, [[Gertrude Robinson|Gertrude]] discovers this plan and tips off the Guardian newspaper, and the building receives much support from the city by being dubbed "The Loneliest Building in Britain." Though this ruins his ritual, now that he had had a taste of "the game," [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] was easily persuaded by [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]]/[[Jonah Magnus|Jonah]] to take over the [[The Magnus Institute|Institute]] by promising him [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]].

# Post-Statement

[[Peter Lukas|Peter]] states that [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] had won, and that his prize is [[Jonathan Sims|John]]. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] tries to compel him to tell him what [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] plans to do with him, but [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] resists. With a boom of static, [[Peter Lukas|Peter]] screams and disappears -- [[Jonathan Sims|John]] calls him stubborn, and it seems that [[Jonathan Sims|John]]'s powers have killed him.

After, [[Jonathan Sims|John]] finds [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] again, who seems to be unable to see him. He once again begs [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] to come with him, who refuses. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] asks him to look at him, and tell him what he sees. After a moment, [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] says "I see you, [[Jonathan Sims|John]]", and begins to laugh with joy. [[Jonathan Sims|John]] tells him that he knows the way home, and leads them out of [[The Lonely]].

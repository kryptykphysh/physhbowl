# Statement

[[Adonis Biros|Biros]] has loved acting ever since his parents took him to see the great amphitheatre near Athens. He particularly enjoys monologues; they give him the sense that he is alone in the universe, speaking from the core of himself. He has become a moderately successful actor, but he views most of his work as necessary annoyances to get him to a good monologue.

In 2009 he played Jacques in a production of _As You Like It_, which allowed him to perform the Seven Ages of Man monologue. Typically he was unable to see much of anything in the crowd due to the bright stage lights. However, on 16 August, he noticed a white porcelain mask was visible in the audience. It sat atop a thick, black cloak, but [[Adonis Biros|Biros]] was certain that there was not a person underneath it.

The next night, [[Adonis Biros|Biros]] did not see the mask in the theatre, but as he was taking a walk through central London after the show to relax he saw the mask in a second-story window. [[Adonis Biros|Biros]] became frightened and ran away, but streets he expected to be full of people were deserted, and he kept seeing the mask-and-cloak figure behind him every time he looked back. Eventually, too exhausted to keep running, [[Adonis Biros|Biros]] confronted the masked figure and struck it. The whole shape collapsed, confirming there was nothing holding the mask up but a wooden stick.

On the night of the 19th, [[Adonis Biros|Biros]] reports that the theatre gradually filled with masks, until every seat was taken by one. After the show, the director came to congratulate [[Adonis Biros|Biros]] on his performance, but [[Adonis Biros|Biros]] became convinced that the director was, ultimately, just a mask himself, hollow on the inside.

He is meant to give his next performance four hours after giving this statement, and he knows he will again be performing for a crowd of hollow masks. However, he does not think he can just run, as "all the world's a stage."

# Post-Statement

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] remarks that recording the statements is getting easier. He also mentions that [[Timothy Stoker|Tim]] has been away from the Archive, "working on something" with frightening intensity, and that [[Melanie king|Melanie]] seems "off" and has not been doing much work. He resolves to ask [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] about it as she and [[Melanie King|Melanie]] are somewhat close.

[[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] is interrupted by the sudden appearance of [[Peter Lukas]], who greets him warmly and suggests they have a chat. [[Peter Lukas|Lukas]] asks how [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] finds [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] as a boss, and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] answers fairly honestly that the spying, murder and unsettling encounters are not what he looks for in a supervisor. [[Peter Lukas|Lukas]] promises to discuss it with [[Elias Bouchard|Elias]] and leaves.

After [[Peter Lukas|Lukas]] leaves, [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] enters the office. She and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] briefly discuss [[Peter Lukas]], then she talks about the reading she's been doing recently on [[The People's Church of the Divine Host]]. She mentions that around the same time as the statement in [[MAG 25 - Growing Dark]], there was a total solar eclipse in [[Ny-Ålesund]], and the last one had been 300 years ago, like how long [[Natalie Ennis]] had described the church waiting for. She then begins to say something about the relationship between [[Edmond Halley]] and [[John Flamsteed]], but [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] cuts her off and asks about [[Melanie King|Melanie]]. [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] knows something is up with [[Melanie King|Melanie]] as well, and that it is connected to her meeting with Elias a few weeks ago. She and [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] start discussing what to do, but [[Basira Hussain|Basira]] reminds [[Martin Blackwood|Martin]] to turn off the tape recorder first.
